# Growth Driver Tree

## Category
Growth / Business Driver Analysis

## Purpose
Decompose business growth into measurable drivers so the business can identify which growth levers matter most.

## Core Question
Which underlying drivers explain the growth gap, and which levers have the greatest potential impact?

## Use When
- Revenue or customer growth is below expectations.
- Management needs to identify the largest growth levers.
- Several acquisition, conversion, retention, pricing, or expansion drivers may be involved.
- A broad growth problem needs to be narrowed into measurable branches.
- Management needs to prioritize growth initiatives.

## Do NOT Use When
- The problem has no meaningful measurable growth drivers.
- The objective is purely qualitative.
- A specific driver has already been isolated.
- Available data is insufficient to evaluate important branches.
- The tree would simply reproduce organizational departments.

- Growth
- Customer
- Marketing

- Profitability

## Decision Types
- Strategic
- Optimization

## Key Questions
- What is the target growth outcome?
- What are the major drivers of that outcome?
- Which drivers explain the current gap?
- Which drivers have the largest impact?
- Which drivers are controllable?
- Which drivers can change quickly?
- Which drivers require structural investment?
- Which drivers interact with one another?
- What evidence is required for each branch?
- Which branch should be investigated first?

## Required Inputs
- Revenue or customer growth target
- Historical growth
- Customer counts
- Acquisition volume
- Conversion rates
- Retention or churn
- Average transaction value
- Revenue per customer
- Expansion or cross-sell data
- Channel performance
- Product or segment performance

## Analysis Process
1. Define the growth outcome.
2. Establish the major measurable drivers.
3. Break important drivers into deeper components.
4. Check that the branches are logically distinct.
5. Quantify each branch where possible.
6. Compare actual performance with target performance.
7. Identify the largest gaps.
8. Identify the highest-potential growth levers.
9. Prioritize branches for deeper investigation.
10. Apply specialized frameworks to the selected branches.

## Expected Output
- A structured growth driver tree.
- Quantified growth gaps.
- Prioritized growth levers.
- Hypotheses for deeper analysis.
- A clear analytical sequence.

## Interpretation
The most useful tree identifies a small number of measurable drivers that explain a meaningful portion of the growth outcome. Priority should depend on impact, evidence, controllability, and potential upside.

## Limitations
- Drivers may interact.
- A tree is a model of the business, not reality itself.
- Poor data can create misleading conclusions.
- Excessive detail can obscure the important drivers.
- A driver relationship does not automatically establish causality.

## Common Mistakes
- Organizing branches by departments instead of business drivers.
- Making the tree too detailed too early.
- Creating overlapping branches.
- Treating all drivers as equally important.
- Failing to connect branches to evidence.
- Assuming the tree itself proves causality.

## Compatible Frameworks
- Revenue Decomposition
- Growth Funnel
- Cohort Analysis
- Customer Segmentation
- Pareto Analysis
- Issue Tree
- Unit Economics

## Framework Sequence
- Before: Issue Tree, Revenue Decomposition
- After: Growth Funnel, Cohort Analysis, Customer Segmentation, Unit Economics, Root Cause Analysis

## Decision Impact
High

## Consulting Judgment
Build the tree from the business outcome backward. Prioritize branches based on impact, evidence, controllability, and time-to-effect rather than treating every branch equally.

## Framework Reasoning Rule
Translate a broad growth objective into measurable drivers, then investigate the branches that can materially change the decision.

## Framework Chain
Growth Gap
→ Growth Driver Tree
→ Largest Driver
→ Driver-Specific Analysis
→ Hypothesis Testing
→ Growth Initiative
→ KPI

## Framework Combination Rules
- Use Revenue Decomposition when revenue movement is the starting symptom.
- Use Growth Funnel for acquisition and conversion branches.
- Use Cohort Analysis for retention and customer-quality branches.
- Use Customer Segmentation when performance differs across customer groups.
- Use Unit Economics before recommending growth that may destroy economics.

## Framework Selection Hierarchy
1. Use when management needs to understand where growth comes from.
2. Use early when several growth levers are plausible.
3. Move to specialized frameworks once a dominant branch is identified.
4. Stop expanding the tree when additional detail no longer changes the decision.
