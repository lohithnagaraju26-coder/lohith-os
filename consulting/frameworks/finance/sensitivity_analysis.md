# Sensitivity Analysis

## Category
Finance / Strategy / Decision Analysis

## Purpose
Determine how strongly a business outcome changes when important assumptions or variables change.

## Core Question
Which assumptions have the greatest influence on the decision or business outcome?

## Use When
- A decision depends on several numerical assumptions.
- Management needs to know which variables matter most.
- The robustness of an investment or market-entry case is uncertain.
- Management wants to identify critical assumptions before making a decision.
- A financial model contains assumptions that could vary materially.

## Do NOT Use When
- The outcome cannot be meaningfully quantified.
- There are no important variable assumptions.
- The objective is to model several interacting future worlds rather than isolate variable impact.
- The inputs are so uncertain that precise numerical sensitivity would create false confidence.
- A qualitative strategic question is being treated as a purely numerical problem.

## Key Questions
- What is the target outcome?
- Which assumptions drive the outcome?
- Which variables can be changed independently?
- Which variables have the largest impact?
- What happens when each critical variable increases or decreases?
- Which variables cause the recommendation to change?
- What is the break-even value for critical assumptions?
- Which assumptions are controllable?
- Which assumptions are external?
- Which assumptions require the most accurate information?
- Where is the decision most fragile?
- Which assumptions deserve further research?

## Required Inputs
- Base-case financial model
- Revenue assumptions
- Pricing assumptions
- Volume assumptions
- Customer assumptions
- Cost assumptions
- Investment assumptions
- Market growth assumptions
- Discount rate where relevant
- Timing assumptions
- Critical operating assumptions
- Decision threshold

## Analysis Process
1. Define the outcome being tested.
2. Establish the base case.
3. Identify key assumptions.
4. Select the variables most likely to affect the decision.
5. Vary important variables systematically.
6. Measure the effect on the outcome.
7. Rank variables by impact.
8. Identify threshold or break-even values where useful.
9. Determine whether the recommendation changes.
10. Identify which assumptions require better evidence.

## Expected Output
- Ranked sensitivity drivers.
- Effect of changing major assumptions.
- Critical thresholds.
- Break-even points where relevant.
- Identification of fragile assumptions.
- Research priorities for decision-critical uncertainty.

## Interpretation
A strong sensitivity analysis identifies the few assumptions that materially change the business case. Management should focus further research and risk mitigation on those variables.

## Limitations
- Sensitivity analysis often changes one variable at a time and may not capture interactions.
- Results depend on the quality of the underlying model.
- Small numerical differences can appear more important than they are strategically.
- It does not automatically establish probabilities.
- It can create false precision when assumptions are poorly supported.

## Common Mistakes
- Testing too many irrelevant variables.
- Ignoring interactions between variables.
- Using unrealistic ranges.
- Treating sensitivity as probability.
- Failing to identify the decision threshold.
- Focusing on model precision instead of decision relevance.
- Ignoring qualitative factors.

## Decision Impact
High

## Consulting Judgment
Sensitivity analysis should identify where the decision is fragile. Do not spend equal effort on every variable; prioritize assumptions that can change the recommendation.

## Framework Reasoning Rule
Find the variables that can change the decision, not merely the variables that can change the model.

## Framework Chain
Base Case

→ Sensitivity Test
→ Impact Ranking
→ Thresholds
→ Fragility Assessment
→ Research / Risk Mitigation

## Framework Combination Rules
- Use Scenario Analysis to examine combinations of uncertain assumptions.
- Use PESTEL to identify external variables that should be tested.
- Use Break-Even Analysis when minimum viable conditions matter.
- Use Decision Matrix when quantitative sensitivity must be combined with qualitative strategic criteria.
- Use Revenue Decomposition when the sensitive variables relate to revenue drivers.

## Framework Selection Hierarchy
1. Establish a credible base case.
2. Identify decision-critical variables.
3. Test reasonable ranges.
4. Identify variables that change the recommendation.
5. Focus further diligence on those variables.
