# Revenue Decomposition

## Category
Finance / Growth Diagnosis

## Purpose
Break a revenue change into its fundamental business drivers so the analysis can identify exactly where the change originated.

## Core Question
What underlying components explain the change in revenue?

## Use When
- Revenue has increased or decreased unexpectedly.
- Management needs to understand the drivers of revenue growth or decline.
- The business has multiple customer, product, geographic, or channel drivers.
- A revenue problem must be quantified before deeper analysis.
- The business needs to distinguish customer-volume effects from monetization effects.

## Do NOT Use When
- Revenue is not the relevant business outcome.
- Reliable revenue-driver data is unavailable.
- The problem is purely operational and has no meaningful revenue relationship.
- The specific revenue driver is already known and requires a more specialized framework.

## Problem Types
- Growth
- Profitability
- Diagnostic
- Customer
- Marketing
- Product

## Decision Types
- Diagnostic
- Strategic
- Optimization

## Key Questions
- Did the number of customers change?
- Did revenue per customer change?
- Did new customer acquisition change?
- Did customer retention or churn change?
- Did price change?
- Did purchase volume change?
- Did product or service mix change?
- Which driver explains the largest share of the revenue change?
- Which drivers are controllable?
- Which drivers are temporary versus structural?

## Required Inputs
- Historical revenue
- Revenue by period
- Customer count
- New customers
- Retained customers
- Churned customers
- Average revenue per customer
- Pricing data
- Volume data
- Product or service mix
- Channel or geographic mix where relevant

## Analysis Process
1. Establish the revenue baseline and comparison period.
2. Decompose revenue into major mathematical drivers.
3. Separate customer-volume effects from monetization effects.
4. Break major drivers into secondary drivers.
5. Quantify the contribution of each driver.
6. Identify the largest drivers of change.
7. Test whether the observed drivers are temporary or structural.
8. Link major drivers to hypotheses that can be investigated with specialized frameworks.

## Expected Output
- A quantified revenue-driver tree.
- Contribution of each major driver to the overall change.
- Identification of the largest positive and negative drivers.
- A prioritized set of hypotheses for deeper investigation.

## Interpretation
A useful decomposition explains not merely that revenue changed, but which measurable business drivers caused the change.

## Limitations
- Results depend on the quality and granularity of business data.
- Revenue drivers may interact with one another.
- Historical decomposition does not automatically establish causality.
- A large driver may be externally caused and therefore difficult to influence.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating revenue growth as a single undifferentiated number.
- Confusing correlation with causation.
- Double-counting drivers.
- Ignoring product, channel, or customer mix.
- Stopping after identifying a driver without investigating its root cause.

## Compatible Frameworks
- Growth Driver Tree
- Cohort Analysis
- Customer Segmentation
- Growth Funnel
- Profitability Tree
- Root Cause Analysis
- Pareto Analysis

## Framework Sequence
- Before: Issue Tree, MECE
- After: Growth Driver Tree, Cohort Analysis, Customer Segmentation, Growth Funnel

## Decision Impact
High

## Consulting Judgment
Start with decomposition whenever the business problem is a measurable change in an outcome. Quantify the largest drivers before jumping to broad strategic explanations.

## Framework Reasoning Rule
Decompose the outcome into measurable drivers before explaining why those drivers changed.

## Framework Chain
Revenue Change
→ Revenue Decomposition
→ Identify Largest Driver
→ Select Driver-Specific Framework
→ Test Hypothesis
→ Quantify Root Cause
→ Recommend Action

## Framework Combination Rules
- Combine with Growth Driver Tree when customer growth or retention requires deeper decomposition.
- Combine with Cohort Analysis when retention or customer behavior appears to be a major driver.
- Combine with Customer Segmentation when the effect may be concentrated in specific customer groups.
- Combine with Growth Funnel when acquisition or conversion appears to be responsible.
- Combine with Root Cause Analysis after the major quantitative driver has been identified.

## Framework Selection Hierarchy
1. Use when revenue movement is the primary symptom.
2. Use before narrower revenue-driver frameworks unless the specific driver is already known.
3. Move to specialized follow-up frameworks once the dominant driver is identified.
4. Do not continue using it after the relevant driver has been sufficiently isolated.
