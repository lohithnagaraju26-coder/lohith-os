# Build vs Buy

## Category
Technology / Strategy / Investment

## Purpose
Evaluate whether a capability should be developed internally, acquired from an external provider, or implemented through a hybrid approach.

## Core Question
Should the organization build the required capability internally, buy it externally, or combine internal and external solutions?

## Use When
- A business needs a new technology capability.
- AI or automation implementation options are being evaluated.
- Multiple vendors or implementation approaches exist.
- Internal development is possible but requires significant investment.
- The organization needs to balance speed, control, cost, differentiation, and risk.
- Management must make an implementation investment decision.

## Do NOT Use When
- There is only one viable implementation approach.
- The capability has no meaningful strategic or economic impact.
- The question is whether the technology is technically feasible.
- Reliable cost, capability, or vendor information is unavailable.
- Build and buy alternatives are not actually comparable.

## Key Questions
- What capability is required?
- Is the capability strategically differentiating?
- Does the organization already have the required skills?
- How quickly must the capability be available?
- What is the total cost of building?
- What is the total cost of buying?
- What are the ongoing operating costs?
- What level of customization is required?
- How important is control over the technology?
- What security and compliance requirements exist?
- What are the vendor dependencies?
- How difficult would it be to switch vendors later?
- Does the capability require proprietary data or processes?
- What is the expected life of the solution?
- What are the implementation risks?
- Which option provides the best risk-adjusted value?

## Analysis Process
1. Define the capability and required outcome.
2. Establish must-have requirements.
3. Assess internal capabilities.
4. Identify credible external alternatives.
5. Estimate total cost of ownership for each option.
6. Compare implementation speed.
7. Compare customization and control.
8. Assess vendor and technology risks.
9. Assess security, compliance, and integration requirements.
10. Assess strategic differentiation.
11. Evaluate switching and lock-in risks.
12. Compare options using explicit decision criteria.
13. Recommend build, buy, hybrid, or defer.

## Expected Output
- Build vs buy comparison.
- Total cost comparison.
- Capability and timeline comparison.
- Risk assessment.
- Strategic implications.
- Recommended implementation approach.

## Interpretation
Buying generally becomes more attractive when speed, mature capability, and lower internal complexity matter most. Building becomes more attractive when the capability is strategically differentiating, requires deep customization, or depends heavily on proprietary processes or data. Hybrid approaches may provide the best balance.

## Limitations
- Cost estimates may be uncertain.
- Vendor capabilities change over time.
- Strategic value can be difficult to quantify.
- Switching costs may be underestimated.
- Short-term cost advantages may differ from long-term economics.

## Common Mistakes
- Comparing purchase price instead of total cost.
- Ignoring implementation and integration costs.
- Ignoring internal opportunity cost.
- Assuming vendor capabilities remain constant.
- Treating strategic differentiation as purely financial.
- Ignoring vendor lock-in.
- Ignoring security and compliance.
- Choosing build simply because internal development is technically possible.

## Consulting Judgment
Do not start with build versus buy. First establish the business requirement, feasibility, strategic importance, and constraints. Compare alternatives using total cost, speed, control, differentiation, risk, and reversibility.

## Framework Reasoning Rule
Choose the implementation approach that maximizes risk-adjusted business value rather than minimizing upfront cost.

## Framework Combination Rules
- Use AI Feasibility Assessment before comparing AI implementation options.
- Use Agent Suitability Assessment when an autonomous agent is being considered.
- Use TCO to compare lifetime costs.
- Use Decision Matrix when qualitative and quantitative criteria must be combined.
- Use Technology Maturity when vendor or internal capability maturity is uncertain.

## Framework Selection Hierarchy
1. Define the required capability.
2. Validate feasibility.
3. Assess strategic differentiation.
4. Compare build, buy, and hybrid alternatives.
5. Evaluate total cost and risk.
6. Select the most reversible option when uncertainty is high.

## Quality Standard
The decision must compare realistic alternatives using explicit criteria, total cost, strategic importance, implementation speed, risks, and long-term implications.
