# Porter's Five Forces

## Category
Strategy / Market Entry / Industry Analysis

## Purpose
Assess the structural attractiveness and competitive intensity of an industry by examining the forces that influence industry economics and profitability.

## Core Question
How attractive is the industry structure, and what forces determine the intensity of competition and potential profitability?

## Use When
- Entering a new market or industry.
- Assessing industry attractiveness.
- Understanding competitive pressure.
- Evaluating whether industry economics support attractive returns.
- Comparing industries or market segments.
- Developing a competitive strategy.

## Do NOT Use When
- The question is primarily about macroeconomic conditions.
- The industry boundaries are poorly defined.
- The analysis requires detailed company-level execution assessment.
- The market is too new or rapidly changing for structural forces to be meaningfully assessed.
- The framework would merely produce a list of competitors without analyzing industry economics.

## Key Questions
- How intense is rivalry among existing competitors?
- How easy is it for new competitors to enter?
- How much bargaining power do suppliers have?
- How much bargaining power do customers have?
- How strong is the threat of substitutes?
- Which force has the greatest effect on industry attractiveness?
- Are competitors differentiated or offering similar products?
- Is industry growth high or low?
- Are there significant barriers to entry?
- Are switching costs high or low?
- Are suppliers concentrated?
- Are customers concentrated?
- Are credible substitutes readily available?
- How sustainable are current industry economics?
- How could the forces change over time?

## Analysis Process
1. Define the relevant industry boundary.
2. Identify major competitors and industry participants.
3. Assess the intensity of rivalry.
4. Assess barriers and threat of new entrants.
5. Assess supplier bargaining power.
6. Assess customer bargaining power.
7. Assess threat of substitutes.
8. Determine which forces most strongly affect industry economics.
9. Identify how forces interact.
10. Assess whether the structure is favorable or unfavorable.
11. Consider how the forces could evolve.
12. Translate structural findings into implications for the business strategy.

## Expected Output
- Five-forces industry assessment.
- Relative intensity of each force.
- Key drivers of industry attractiveness.
- Structural opportunities and threats.
- Implications for market entry and competitive strategy.

## Interpretation
A structurally attractive industry generally has weaker competitive forces and better conditions for sustainable economics. Strong forces do not automatically make entry impossible, but they indicate that the entrant needs a clear source of advantage.

## Limitations
- Industry boundaries can be subjective.
- The framework provides structural analysis rather than company-specific execution analysis.
- Rapid technological change can alter industry structure quickly.
- The framework is less useful when industry economics are highly unstable.
- It does not quantify the financial attractiveness of a specific investment by itself.

## Common Mistakes
- Defining the industry too broadly or narrowly.
- Treating competitors as the only force.
- Ignoring substitutes.
- Ignoring customer and supplier concentration.
- Assuming high industry growth automatically means high attractiveness.
- Treating the forces as independent when they interact.
- Failing to translate industry structure into strategic implications.

## Consulting Judgment
Define the competitive arena carefully before applying the framework. Focus on the structural forces that determine economics rather than simply describing competitors. Use the output to understand whether the company can establish a defensible position.

## Framework Reasoning Rule
Assess the forces that shape industry economics, not merely the visible competitors.

## Framework Combination Rules
- Combine with PESTEL to separate macro-environmental forces from industry structure.
- Combine with Scenario Analysis when industry structure may change under different future conditions.
- Combine with Sensitivity Analysis when specific structural assumptions can be quantified.
- Combine with Decision Matrix when multiple markets or industries are being compared.
- Combine with Value Chain when the company needs to determine where it can build an advantage within the industry.

## Framework Selection Hierarchy
1. Define the industry boundary.
2. Assess each of the five forces.
3. Identify the forces most relevant to industry economics.
4. Determine whether the structure is attractive or difficult.
5. Translate the structural assessment into strategic implications.
6. Combine with financial and capability analysis before making an entry decision.

## Quality Standard
The analysis must clearly define the industry, assess all five forces with evidence, identify the forces that matter most, consider their interactions, and translate structural findings into strategic consequences.
