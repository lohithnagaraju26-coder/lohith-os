# LOHITH OS — BUSINESS PROBLEM TAXONOMY

## Purpose

This taxonomy teaches LOHITH OS to identify the type of business problem before selecting frameworks, analysis methods, or recommendations.

LOHITH OS should never begin with:

"What framework should I use?"

It should begin with:

"What is the business problem?"
"What decision needs to be made?"
"What outcome matters?"
"What type of problem is this?"
"What evidence is required?"

The taxonomy provides the first classification layer of the consulting brain.

---

# 1. STRATEGY PROBLEMS

## Definition

Problems involving the direction, positioning, competitive advantage, market choice, growth strategy, or long-term direction of a business.

## Typical Questions

- Where should the company compete?
- Which markets should we enter?
- Which customers should we target?
- How should we differentiate?
- What should our strategy be?
- Should we expand or remain focused?
- What should we stop doing?
- Where should we invest?
- How can we build sustainable competitive advantage?

## Typical Signals

- Growth has slowed.
- Competition is increasing.
- The company is entering a new market.
- Leadership is uncertain about direction.
- Multiple strategic options exist.
- Resources are limited.
- The company needs to choose between opportunities.

## Common Analytical Tools

- Market analysis
- Competitive analysis
- SWOT
- PESTEL
- Porter's Five Forces
- Value chain analysis
- Business model analysis
- Ansoff Matrix
- Strategic positioning
- Scenario analysis
- Attractiveness vs capability analysis

## Core Decision

"Where and how should the company compete?"

---

# 2. GROWTH PROBLEMS

## Definition

Problems involving increasing revenue, customers, market share, usage, transactions, or other growth metrics.

## Typical Questions

- How can we grow revenue?
- How can we acquire more customers?
- Why has growth slowed?
- Which growth lever should we prioritize?
- Which market segment has the most potential?
- How can we increase conversion?
- Should we launch a new product or market?

## Typical Growth Equation

Growth may be decomposed into:

Revenue = Customers × Average Revenue per Customer

or:

Revenue = Traffic × Conversion Rate × Average Order Value

or:

Growth = Acquisition × Activation × Retention × Monetization

The exact decomposition should depend on the business model.

## Common Analytical Tools

- Growth decomposition
- Market sizing
- TAM / SAM / SOM
- Customer segmentation
- Funnel analysis
- Cohort analysis
- Conversion analysis
- Ansoff Matrix
- Growth driver tree
- Customer lifetime value analysis

## Core Decision

"Which growth levers should the company pursue?"

# 3. PROFITABILITY PROBLEMS

## Definition

Problems involving declining profit, margins, costs, pricing, unit economics, or financial performance.

## Typical Questions

- Why is profit declining?
- Why are margins falling?
- Which products are profitable?
- Where are costs increasing?
- Should we raise prices?
- Should we reduce costs?
- Which customers generate the most profit?
- Is the business model economically viable?

## Typical Profit Equation

Profit = Revenue - Cost

Revenue may be decomposed into:

Revenue = Price × Volume

Costs may be decomposed into:

Total Cost = Fixed Costs + Variable Costs

## Common Analytical Tools

- Profitability tree
- Revenue decomposition
- Cost decomposition
- Contribution margin
- Unit economics
- Break-even analysis
- Pricing analysis
- Customer profitability

- Cost benchmarking

## Core Decision

"How can we improve sustainable profitability?"

# 17. MULTI-DOMAIN PROBLEMS

## Definition

Complex business problems that span multiple categories.

Many real-world business problems are not purely one type.

For example:

"Revenue has fallen by 20%."

This may involve:

- Strategy
- Customer
- Marketing
- Product
- Pricing
- Operations
- Competition

LOHITH OS should therefore allow multiple classifications.

## Rule

Do not force every problem into one category.

Instead identify:

### Primary Problem Type

The category most directly related to the central business decision.

### Secondary Problem Types

Additional categories that materially influence the problem.

Example:

Primary:
Profitability

Secondary:
Pricing
Marketing
Customer
Operations

# 18. PROBLEM CLASSIFICATION PROCESS

Before selecting frameworks, LOHITH OS should perform the following classification:

## Step 1 — Identify the business objective

What outcome is the business trying to achieve?

Examples:

- Increase revenue
- Improve profit
- Reduce cost
- Enter a market
- Improve customer retention
- Automate a process
- Launch a product
- Improve operational efficiency

---

## Step 2 — Identify the problem statement

What is currently going wrong?

Example:

"Revenue growth has fallen from 20% to 5%."

---

## Step 3 — Identify the decision

What decision does management need to make?

Example:

"Which growth levers should we prioritize?"

---

## Step 4 — Identify the primary problem type

Classify the problem.

Example:

Primary:
Growth

---

## Step 5 — Identify secondary problem types

Determine contributing domains.

Example:

Secondary:

- Customer
- Marketing
- Product
- Pricing

---

## Step 6 — Identify the problem structure

Determine whether the problem is:

- Diagnostic
- Predictive
- Strategic
- Optimization
- Decision-making
- Implementation
- Transformation

# 20. CLASSIFICATION CONFIDENCE

LOHITH OS should assign a confidence level to its classification.

## High Confidence

The problem clearly belongs to one category.

Example:

"Reduce manufacturing defects by 30%."

Classification:

Primary: Operations
Confidence: High

---

## Medium Confidence

Multiple categories are plausible.

Example:

"Revenue has declined significantly."

Possible categories:

Growth
Customer
Marketing
Pricing
Product

LOHITH OS should ask clarifying questions or conduct additional analysis.

---

## Low Confidence

The problem statement is too vague.

Example:

"Our business isn't performing well."

LOHITH OS should not immediately recommend frameworks.

It should clarify:

- What does "not performing well" mean?
- Revenue?
- Profit?
- Customers?
- Operations?
- Cash flow?
- Market share?

# 22. IMPORTANT RULE

Problem classification is not the final diagnosis.

Classification is the starting point.

LOHITH OS must remain willing to change its classification when evidence reveals that the original interpretation was incorrect.

The system should distinguish between:

Initial classification

and

Evidence-based diagnosis.

---

# 23. CONSULTING PRINCIPLE

Never confuse the symptom with the problem.

Example:

Symptom:

"Sales are falling."

Possible underlying problems:

- Market demand is shrinking
- Competitor gained market share
- Prices are too high
- Product quality declined
- Customer acquisition weakened
- Customer retention declined
- Sales team productivity declined
- Distribution changed

Therefore:

Symptom ≠ Root Cause

LOHITH OS must investigate before recommending action.
